Training Plan

I built this because I couldn't find a training program that worked the way I wanted. I was looking for something hybrid, functional, and most importantly, fun. I'm not the kind of person who enjoys going to the gym just to train two muscle groups and leave.

I enjoy training in different formats — mixing strength, conditioning, running, power, and functional work. The kind of training that keeps you motivated and actually feels good to do. This is how I program my own training.

I spent 4 years doing CrossFit, now I'm into HYROX — and in the future, who knows. For now, these are my sessions. I hope you enjoy them.

Follow the 4-week progression if you want structure. Pick from Standalone Sessions if you just need something for today. Use Train by Mood when your energy decides for you. The program works either way.

Missed a session?
Skip it and continue from where you left off. Don't double up. Don't feel guilty. Just go again.

How to combine gym + running

Run before gym when the run is easy or zone 2 — it warms you up without interfering. Run after gym when it's intervals or tempo — you want full leg power for lifting first. Never run hard the day before a heavy legs session.

All paces calculated from your threshold of 4:08/km. If your fitness changes, adjust proportionally. The feel matters more than the exact number.

Glossary

Terms used throughout this programme — if something was unclear, find it here.

TermWhat it means
AMRAPAs Many Rounds As Possible. Set a timer, do as many complete rounds as you can. Stop when time is up.
Cluster setBreak one set into mini-sets with short intra-set rest. Example: 6 reps, 10 seconds rest, 6 reps, 10 seconds rest, 6 reps. Lets you use more weight than a straight set.
Compromised runRunning while already fatigued from another exercise — exactly what HYROX requires. Builds the ability to maintain pace under fatigue.
DeloadA planned reduction in training volume and intensity (usually 60% load). Allows your nervous system and muscles to recover and consolidate adaptations.
Density supersetTwo exercises performed back to back with minimal rest. Creates more work in less time without increasing session length.
Eccentric overloadSlowing down the lowering phase of a movement. Example: 5 seconds down on bench press. Creates more muscle damage and drives hypertrophy.
EMOMEvery Minute On the Minute. Start a set at the top of each minute. Rest for whatever time is left before the next minute begins.
FOR TIMEComplete all prescribed work as fast as possible. Record your time. Your score is how long it took.
RPERate of Perceived Exertion. Scale of 1–10. RPE 7 = hard but sustainable. RPE 9 = near maximum. RPE 10 = absolute maximum.
SupersetTwo exercises performed back to back before resting. Can be same muscle group (pump) or opposing groups (efficiency).
Tempo (3-1-1)Controls the speed of each phase. First number = eccentric (lowering), second = pause, third = concentric (lifting). 3-1-1 = 3s down, 1s pause, 1s up.
Threshold paceThe pace you can sustain for approximately 60 minutes at maximum effort. Your reference threshold is 4:08/km.
Zone 2Aerobic training zone where you can hold a full conversation. Builds your aerobic base — the foundation of all endurance performance.
